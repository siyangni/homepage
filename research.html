Research

Research beyond the dissertation

Outside the dissertation, I work on two questions: what adolescent vaping signals about other substance use, and how parental migration relates to delinquency.

As in the dissertation, both projects look past a single measurement or a group average to see how patterns differ over time and across subgroups.

Research area

Adolescent substance use

Two studies of US 12th-grade students on how nicotine vaping relates to smoking and to other substance use.

Under review·Nicotine & Tobacco Research

As adolescent vaping declined, it became increasingly differentiated by concurrent non-tobacco substance use rather than by cigarette smoking. Screening and prevention should assess polysubstance use, not smoking alone.

“Is Adolescent Nicotine Vaping Increasingly a Marker of Broader Substance Use? A Temporal Block-Shapley Analysis of US 12th-Grade Students, 2017–2024.”

Ni, Siyang

Under review·Nicotine & Tobacco Research

After matching, past-month smoking probability was 4.3% among post-ENDS youth versus 17.9% among pre-ENDS youth, with a sixteen-fold spread across ENDS-use subgroups.

“Adolescent Cigarette Smoking Before and After the Proliferation of Electronic Nicotine Delivery Systems: A Period-Based Matching Design.”

Staff, Jeremy, Eric P. Baumer, and Siyang Ni

Research area

Parental migration, strain, and delinquency

China’s internal migration leaves tens of millions of children behind in rural households while one or both parents work in cities. I began this work in my M.Phil. thesis at the Chinese University of Hong Kong. I ask not whether these children fare worse on average, but which kinds of parental absence matter and which strains are linked to delinquency.

Under review·Journal of Research in Crime and Delinquency

Adolescents with one migrant parent, not two, reported higher expected delinquency than those with no migrant parent. Migration status conditions which strains link to delinquency.

“When One Migrant Parent Matters More Than Two: Parental Migration, Social Strain, and Delinquency among Adolescents in Rural China.”

Ni, Siyang

Analysis complete; in writing

“Exploring the Gendered Association Between Social Strain, Strain-Induced Negative Emotions, and Delinquency among Left-Behind Children in Rural China: A Test of General Strain Theory.”

Ni, Siyang
