Siyang Ni

I study how self-control develops from childhood through adolescence, and how that development shapes delinquency, substance use, and contact with the justice system.

My work draws on longitudinal models, quasi-experimental designs, and machine learning. I also write open-source software so other researchers can run advanced longitudinal models without proprietary tools.

Course, Not Type

Self-Control Development From Childhood to Adolescence and Criminal Justice Contact at Age 23

How a child’s self-control changed between ages 5 and 17 predicts formal contact with the criminal justice system by age 23. The data show no distinct high-risk group of children behind that prediction. Risk differs by degree, not by kind of child.
